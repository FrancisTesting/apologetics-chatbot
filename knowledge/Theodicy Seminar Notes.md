## IF GOD, WHY EVIL?
Post-Seminar Notes – (Based on the Glory Apologetics Module)

## Introduction
The problem of evil is one of the most important and challenging questions in
philosophy, theology, and everyday life. It is not merely an intellectual issue but also
deeply emotional and spiritual. As explored in the seminar, people approach this
question from three angles:
- Intellectual: Why does evil exist?
- Emotional: Why did this happen to me?
- Spiritual: Can suffering be redeemed?
This study aims to provide a clear, logical, and biblical understanding of evil—its nature,
origin, persistence, purpose, and ultimate defeat.

- The Nature of Evil
A foundational step is understanding what evil actually is.
## What Evil Is Not
- Evil is not a substance
- Evil is not a created “thing”
- Evil is not equal and opposite to good
## What Evil Is
Evil is best understood as a corruption of something good.
## Examples:
- Rust corrupts metal
- Rot corrupts a tree
- Disease corrupts the body
Thus, evil does not exist independently—it exists in something good that has been
damaged.





## Key Concept: Privation
Evil is not mere absence but privation—a lack of something that should be present.
- Blindness = lack of sight in a being meant to see
- Moral evil = lack of righteousness in a being meant to do good
## Important Conclusion
- God created only good things
- Evil is not a “thing”
- Therefore, God did not create evil

- The Origin of Evil
If God created everything good, then how did evil arise?
## The Logical Problem
- God is perfect
- God created everything
- Evil exists
Does that mean God created evil?
## The Resolution: Free Will
God created free beings, and freedom includes the ability to choose otherwise.
- Freedom is a good gift
- But it makes evil possible
## Key Insight
- God made evil possible
- Free creatures made evil actual
## Biblical Explanation
- Evil first originated with Lucifer (Satan)
- Then entered humanity through Adam and Eve







Self-Causation
A free act is self-caused:
- Not caused by God
- Not uncaused
- Caused by the agent itself

- The Persistence of Evil
Why does evil still exist today?
## The Classical Argument
- If God is all-good → He wants to eliminate evil
- If God is all-powerful → He can eliminate evil
- Evil exists → Therefore, God does not exist
## The Response
The argument fails because it assumes:
- Evil is never defeated
But the correct understanding is:
- Evil is not yet defeated
## Theistic Conclusion
- God will defeat evil
- His nature guarantees it:
o He is all-good → wants to
o He is all-powerful → able to

- God’s Sovereignty and Evil
A common confusion: If God is in control, is He the author of evil?
## Clarification
- God permits evil (permissive will)
- God does not promote evil (perfect will)





Illustration: Author and Story
Like an author writing a story:
- The villain acts freely
- The author is not morally responsible for evil actions
- The author ensures the final victory of good
## Key Truth
God allows evil but:
- Remains in control
- Uses it for a greater purpose
- Will ultimately defeat it

- The Purpose of Evil
Why does God allow suffering?
## The Objection
Some suffering appears:
## • Meaningless
## • Unjust
## • Unnecessary
## The Response
Just because we do not know a reason, does not mean there is no reason.
## Our Limitation
- We do not know everything
- We do not see the end
- God does both
## Illustration: The Farmer Story
Events that seem bad may later result in good. Our perspective is limited.







## 6. What We Know About Suffering
Even if we do not know all reasons, we do know some purposes:
- Pain as Protection
- Warns of danger (fire, illness)
- Preserves life
- Pain as Teacher
- Deep lessons come through suffering
- Builds wisdom and maturity
## 3. Character Development
- Courage requires danger
- Patience requires hardship
- Forgiveness requires wrongdoing
## 4. Greater Good
God can bring good out of evil:
- Example: Joseph (Genesis 50:20)
- Temporary suffering → lasting good
## Key Principle
God is more concerned with:
- Character than comfort
- Holiness than happiness

## 7. The Moral Argument
When people say:
“This is unjust”
They assume a standard of justice
## Logical Implication
- No ultimate justice → no ultimate injustice
- But we recognize real injustice
- Therefore, there must be a moral standard




## Conclusion
- A moral law implies a Moral Law Giver (God)
- Thus, the problem of evil actually points to God, not away from Him

- Could God Have Made a Better World?
## Common Alternatives Examined
- No world at all
o Not better—nothing cannot be compared to something
- A world without freedom
o Not moral—no real love or goodness
- Free world without possibility of sin
o Logical contradiction
- Free world where no one sins
o May not be actually possible
## Key Insight
This may not be the best possible world, but:
It may be the best way to reach the best world

- The Problem of Physical Evil
What about natural disasters and suffering not caused directly by humans?
## The Claim
Physical evil cannot be explained by free will.
## The Response
Physical evil is connected to free will in multiple ways:
10 Sources of Physical Evil
- Direct self-harm
- Indirect self-harm
- Others’ direct actions
- Others’ indirect actions
- Natural processes (e.g., floods from rain)



- Greater physical good
- Greater moral good
- Divine discipline
- Fallen world (Adam’s sin)
- Evil spiritual beings
## Conclusion
Free will provides a comprehensive explanation of physical evil.

## 10. Why God Doesn’t Stop All Evil
## Key Answer
God cannot eliminate evil without eliminating freedom
## Why Freedom Matters
- No freedom → no love
- No freedom → no moral goodness
## If God Intervened Constantly:
- No consequences → no learning
- No moral responsibility
- No real choices

## 11. How God Will Defeat Evil
God does not destroy evil immediately—but He will defeat it ultimately.
## Final Defeat Includes:
- Separation of good and evil
- Punishment of evil
- Reward of good
- Defeat of death and Satan
## Two Stages
- First Coming of Christ → official defeat
- Second Coming → complete and final defeat




## Final Conclusion
The problem of evil is not a contradiction but a deep reality that points toward truth:
- Evil is real—but not ultimate
- God allows evil—but controls it
- Suffering exists—but has purpose
- Justice seems delayed—but is guaranteed
## Ultimate Hope
An all-good, all-powerful, all-knowing God ensures:
Evil will not have the final word.
