GLORY APOLOGETICS INDIA – SEMINAR NOTES
This section is explaining different reasons why suffering or physical evil can happen in the world. The point is that suffering does not always have one single cause; it can come from many different sources. These ten points try to show that physical evil can be explained in several ways within a Christian worldview.
Here is the meaning in simple terms:
1. Some suffering is caused by our own actions.
Example: damaging our health by abusing our body.
2. Some suffering comes indirectly from our own choices.
Example: laziness leading to poverty.
3. Some suffering is caused by the direct actions of other people.
Example: abuse or violence.
4. Some suffering happens indirectly because of other people’s choices.
Example: children suffering because of a parent’s alcoholism.
5. Some suffering is a side effect of natural processes that are generally good.
Example: rain helps grow crops, but sometimes it causes floods; wind cleans the air but sometimes causes storms.
6. Some suffering exists because of how the physical world works.
Example: animals survive by eating other animals (the food chain).
7. Some suffering helps produce moral growth.
Example: hardship can build character, patience, or courage.
8. Some suffering may be God’s discipline for wrongdoing.
The Bible sometimes describes God allowing suffering as correction for sin (for example in **First Epistle to the Corinthians 11:30).
9. Some suffering comes from the fallen state of the world caused by human sin.
According to the Bible, sin entered the world through Adam, which affected all creation (Genesis 3; Romans 5).
10. Some suffering may be caused by evil spiritual beings.
The Bible sometimes shows spiritual forces causing harm, such as in the story of **Job.
The main idea:
When people ask why suffering exists, the answer is not always simple. Suffering can come from human choices, natural processes, spiritual forces, or the fallen condition of the world. These different explanations together help account for the reality of physical evil.
